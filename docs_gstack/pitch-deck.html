Fractional Real Estate Investment for Nigeria's Missing Middle

by Nulo Africa · September 2026

01 / 08

The Problem

  • Middle-income professionals in Nigeria (earning ₦500K–₦1M/month) cannot access quality income-producing real estate — entry prices for Abuja residential property are ₦50M–₦150M
  • Their savings lose 10–15% real purchasing power per year in fixed deposits while inflation runs at 25–30%
  • Informal pooling (ajo/esusu) works for small groups but breaks down at ₦50M+ deals: trust does not scale, legal structure is absent, and there is no exit mechanism
02 / 08

The Solution

  • NEST enables a defined group of pre-screened investors to co-own a single, named, income-producing Abuja property — starting from ₦500,000
  • Every fee is disclosed as a line item before investment. Every risk factor is shown. Projected yield (7–9%) is labelled “PROJECTION ONLY — NOT GUARANTEED”
  • The platform enforces: server-side authorization, atomic transactions with an immutable BigInt kobo ledger, mandatory 6-checkbox risk consent, and a hard stop at the ₦100M funding target
